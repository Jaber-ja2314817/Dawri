# TREATMENT — Dawri دوري
## Dark Tech Keynote · 3 min · English voiceover · edge-tts · original score

---

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A — Linear journey** (chosen): Follow Fatima from no digital path → check-in → ticket → triage → doctor → pharmacy, with two quick cuts (Ahmed refill, Ravi emergency) and two explanatory asides (safety layers, stack). The script's own structure; the Dark Tech Keynote grammar makes each UI panel expand from the previous one.

**B — The gap → the system**: Open on a void (empty dark stage, one dim element: a front-desk icon, no path forward). Dawri drops into frame; each layer of the system spawns around it additively. Compelling but buries Fatima — the human story disappears into the architecture.

**C — Zoom ladder**: One Arabic character → phone screen → triage room → system diagram → Qatar map → satellite view. Each level unlocks a new fact. Beautiful for a documentary; too abstract for a clinical pitch where the credibility comes from showing what a nurse actually sees.

**Chosen: A**. The linear journey puts a person at the centre and lets the architecture emerge from her story, not the other way round. The two quick cuts (Ahmed, Ravi) add pace at minute 1:24 without breaking the main thread.

---

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline:** A walk-in patient describes her symptoms in Arabic before she arrives, and her exact words are waiting at triage — because Dawri replaced the clipboard with a phone.

**Arc:**
- **Setup (0:00–0:25):** Walk-ins have no digital path. Front desk, triage, doctor, pharmacy — all manual. The gap.
- **Turn (0:25–1:42):** Fatima walks the whole path on her phone. Chat, map, ticket, nurse, doctor, pharmacy. It works.
- **Complication (1:24–1:42):** Ahmed skips the queue entirely (refill). Ravi gets stopped before he reaches it (emergency).
- **Proof (1:42–2:14):** Why it's safe: five rules, in order, one at a time. The snap grid.
- **Stack (2:14–2:41):** What runs it and why each choice was made.
- **Ending (2:41–3:00):** The aim in three lines, then the catchphrase — written in the accent, alone on black.

---

## 3. Benchmark

**Learn:**
- Apple product reveals: one element per screen, the subject centred at ≥⅓ frame height, light that sweeps from darkness into the accent.
- Linear launch films: 1 px grid lines at ~3% white, every element snapping on the beat, the whole grid invisible until it matters.
- Edgar Wright insert cuts: a half-second close-up that expands from its source, landing on a beat. Used for the ticket number, the allergy tag, the emergency red screen.

**Don't take:** any specific UI, any melody, any character design, any logo, any screen from their products. The Dawri interface is redrawn in code from the screenshots taken at xtamim-dawri.hf.space.

## 8. Subtitle and title design

- **Captions:** Dark Tech Keynote bottom-centre toast — dark translucent pill, 1 px light border, Inter 500 ~40 px, small accent dot on left. Enters with 0.16 s rise+fade.
- **On-screen Arabic:** in a white speech bubble (from the app UI), not subtitle-burned — it's a UI element, not a caption.
- **Safety layer cards:** Inter 600, white, left-aligned inside the dark card, card border 1 px at 8% white.
- **Architecture labels:** JetBrains Mono, label-grey, small-caps wide tracking.
- **Final titles:** "Dawri." in Inter 700, white, 96 px. "Arrive, don't register." in Inter 600, accent `#2563eb`, 72 px.
- **Subtitle .srt:** English voiceover lines only; Arabic on-screen text goes in the .srt as a transcript note, not a timed cue.
