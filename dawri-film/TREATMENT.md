# TREATMENT — Dawri دوري (v2)
## Living Screencast · ~3:05 · English voice (edge-tts, en-US-AndrewNeural) · original score · 30 fps

Why a remake: the first cut (Dark Tech Keynote) put a small phone on a large black grid, left most frames empty, fell back to a serif caption font and invented abstract UI. This version shows **the real Dawri interface, redrawn in code, filling the frame**, on a flat blue desktop, with a camera that moves like a screen recorder and one character that carries the story.

Frame rules taken from the front-end design pass (tasteskill): the subject fills at least a third of the frame and usually two-thirds; two type sizes do the work (UI size and one very large size); one ground, one ink, one accent (the app's own `#1d5fbf`); flat fields of colour, no gradients, no grid lines, no glows; equal outer margins; no frame that is mostly empty ground.

---

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A — The ticket's journey (chosen).** The mascot *is* Dawri's queue ticket: a pixel ticket stub. It is born in the gap where the front desk used to be, rides Fatima's visit from chat to ticket to nurse to doctor to pharmacy, splits in two for Ahmed and Ravi (and is refused for Ravi), walks the safety list, rides the architecture lane, and ends on the wordmark. One continuous desktop, one character, real screens.

**B — Two desks race.** Split screen for the whole film: the paper front desk on the left and Dawri on the right, racing the same visit. Clear, but it would invent a paper desk UI we can't show truthfully, and the script makes no time-saved claims (the handoff forbids claiming minutes saved).

**C — A day at Al Wakra.** Morning to evening at one centre, the wallpaper shifting with the hour, three patients across the day. Lovely, but it bends the script's order (safety and stack would have no home) and suggests real operations, which it is not.

**Chosen: A.** It keeps the verified script order, puts the real interface centre stage, and gives the film one actor whose fate *is* the product: a ticket that appears only when it should, and doesn't when it shouldn't (refill, emergency).

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline:** A little pixel ticket is born in the gap where the front desk used to be, and walks Fatima from her first Arabic sentence to the pharmacy, while the rules keep it away from anyone who shouldn't be in a queue.

- **Setup (0:00–0:27):** The real "Steps of your visit" rail, with an extra paper step in front: Front desk. Two other apps cover appointments and records. A dashed empty slot before the desk: the gap. Silence. The ticket drops into it: *Dawri*.
- **Turn (0:27–1:43):** Fatima's whole visit on the real screens: demo login, the Arabic chat, the map, the reservation, "I'm here", ticket T-014, then a whip to the staff window for the nurse, the doctor (the amoxicillin warning) and the pharmacy.
- **Complication (1:43–2:00):** The phone divides. Ahmed is sent to the refill route with no ticket. Ravi's phone turns red, and the ticket is refused at the door.
- **Proof (2:00–2:36):** The five safety layers, each beside a live example, with the honest amber line last.
- **Stack (2:36–3:07):** The architecture as one lane the ticket rides, the two model cards and the 16-pair test.
- **Ending (3:07–3:27):** Bookend: the step rail returns without the front desk. "Dawri." Silence. The ticket hops across "Arrive, don't register." and settles.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Screen Studio-style recordings:** learn the auto-zoom to the cursor, smoothed cursor and click push-ins, and the window on a flat wallpaper. Don't take any wallpaper, cursor art or UI.
- **Apple "Guided Tour" films:** learn one feature per sentence, real UI and a calm narrator. Don't take any shots or music.
- **Duolingo-style mascot acting:** learn anticipation, squash and stretch, and eyes that do the acting. Don't take any character design: ours is an original pixel ticket stub.

## 5. Beat sheet

The voice lines (v01–v35, `lines.json`) set the clock; `film.js` builds the timeline from the measured durations in `out/voice/dur.json`, and every visual beat hangs on a line start plus an offset. Gaps between lines: 0.35 s inside a thought, 0.9–1.4 s at section turns, **1.6 s of near-silence before "That is the gap."** and **1.4 s before "Dawri."**, the film's two silences.

## 6. Cue map

**96 BPM** (beat = 0.625 s), 4/4. Hi-fi layer: felt/electric piano (Rhodes-like FM), upright-style bass (sine + harmonics), brushed kit (synth noise). Mascot layer: square-wave chip voice.

| Section | Music state |
|---|---|
| 01 The gap | Solo electric piano chords, sparse; drop to room tone before the gap; chip "blip" arpeggio when the ticket lands |
| 02 Fatima | Band in: bass + brushes; chip voice doubles the melody on mascot moves; tom fill + crash at the whip to staff |
| 03 Two paths | Stereo split: Ahmed's side dry piano left, Ravi's side the drums drop, a low pad builds then cuts on red |
| 04 Safety | Drums out, single held pad + soft piano, a chip "tick" on each row |
| 05 Stack | Band back, steady groove, glockenspiel-like FM on cards |
| 06 Ending | Subtract to one piano chord; silence; chip motif on each hopped word; final chord |

Picture cuts and pops snap to the nearest eighth note of the grid where a line allows.
